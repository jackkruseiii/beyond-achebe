## I — Routine

**Weekly:** two weekday sessions of ~60 min + one weekend session of ~2 hrs (4 hrs total). Each session opens with the country's poem (10 min).

**Low-energy floor:** one poem or 10 pages counts as a session. Never skip a session entirely.

**If-then rules (the system's guard rails):**
- **If** I start a book, **then** I confirm the next two books are in hand that day, and order or download them if not. (Pipeline rule — the stall happens when there's no next book.)
- **If** a title can't be obtained within 3 weeks, **then** switch to the listed alternative, no deliberating.
- **If** I finish a book, **then** my next session is the review dictation, not new reading.
- **If** I can't manage the full 25-minute dictation, **then** I dictate prompt 2 only (5 min), and it counts.

**While reading:** at the end of each session, one line in a phone note (what happened; what struck me). Kindle highlights for e-books; phone photo of the page for print.

**Review dictation template (copy into phone notes):**

```
REVIEW DICTATION — ____________ (country)   Finished: ________
Book / author / year:          Poem / poet:
1. The story in two sentences.
2. What moment in this country's history does it put me inside — and what
   did I understand that a history book couldn't give me?
3. The passage I'd teach, and why. (page #)
4. How the poem talks to the novel.
5. Who should read it, and my verdict (rating ___/5).
Course module: ________
```

**What Claude does with the dictation:** fixes punctuation and dictation errors only, lists every change, never rewords. The cleaned text becomes the blog review (Jack posts it), the `books.js` summary (verbatim, after the post is live), and the raw material for the book chapter and course week.

**Publishing:** batch blog posts and site updates monthly if a per-book update isn't convenient.

## Course skeleton (keep, don't build until Newport)

"Beyond Achebe: Africa's History Through Its Fiction" — 14 weeks, each paired with that week's poems.

1. The danger of a single story — *Arrow of God*
2. Oral and precolonial worlds — *Epic of Askia Mohammed*, *Chaka*
3. Slavery and the Atlantic — *Homegoing*, *Doguicimi* / *The Viceroy of Ouidah*, *The Shepherd's House*
4. Conquest and colonial rule — *Houseboy*, *Confines of the Shadow*, *Bound to Violence*
5. Liberation wars — *What the Day Owes the Night*, *The Ultimate Tragedy*, *Sleepwalking Land*
6. Settler states and apartheid — *Long Walk to Freedom*, *Born a Crime*, *We Need New Names*
7. Independence and disillusionment — *A Bend in the River*, *Memoirs of a Porcupine*
8. Africa in the Cold War — *American Spy*, *Beneath the Lion's Gaze*
9. Civil wars and child soldiers — *Allah Is Not Obliged*, *The Memory of Love*, *She Would Be King*
10. Genocide and memory — *Small Country*, the Rwanda memoir
11. The Horn and the Sudans — *Gratitude in Low Voices*, *Edo's Souls*, *Transit*
12. Women, marriage and tradition — *So Long a Letter*, *Awu's Story*, *Co-Wives, Co-Widows*
13. The Indian Ocean — *The Last Brother*, *Beyond the Rice Fields*, *The Kafir of Karthala*
14. Migration and diaspora — *Americanah*, *Behold the Dreamers*, *Leaving Tangier*
